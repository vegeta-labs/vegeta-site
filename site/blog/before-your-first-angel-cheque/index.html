Before your first angel cheque: size the portfolio, not the deal

Angel returns are lopsided enough that the number of investments you can afford matters as much as your choice of the first one.

Updated 7 November 2026

Most first-time angels ask whether a particular company is good. The prior question is how many companies you can back, because one cheque is a bet on an outcome that usually disappoints.

What the data looks like

The best-known evidence is a 2007 study for the Kauffman Foundation, which surveyed 539 angels in investor groups about 1,137 exits. It is old, self-reported and limited to people in organised groups, so take its shape rather than its precise values. The shape is striking:

  • 52% of exits returned less than the money invested.
  • About a tenth of exits produced 75% of the cash returned, and 7% returned more than ten times.
  • The average across all exits was 2.6 times over about 3.5 years, a figure carried by that small group.
  • 39% of the angels had lost money overall on the investments they had exited, not counting holdings still open.

The last point is the one to remember. Holding several investments improved the odds without removing the chance of a loss.

Why the count matters

Treat each investment as an independent draw with a 7% chance of a tenfold-plus result. Real deals are not fully independent, and your picks may be better or worse than the survey, but the sum shows the pattern.

Investments Chance of no tenfold-plus exit
5 70% (0.93⁵)
10 48% (0.93¹⁰)
20 23% (0.93²⁰)

Even at twenty, nearly a quarter of portfolios miss. At one or two, you are almost certainly relying on a smaller win or a loss. Those winners also took the longest: the average hold in the study rose to six years in its top band, and the report notes holds beyond ten years were not unusual among strong performers.

Working out a cheque

Start with a sum you could lose completely without changing how you live, and be strict. Money for a house deposit or school fees does not count. Count any startup equity you already hold through work, since that is the same risk on the same market.

Say Goku settles on £60,000 over three years. Keeping a third back for later rounds leaves £40,000 for first cheques. Ten companies at £4,000 each is the plan. £25,000 minimums cannot deliver that plan: two such cheques overrun the £40,000 and eat into the reserve. Lower-minimum vehicles change access and add their own fees, rights and reliance on a lead. He could also revise the plan or not invest.

The reserve exists because good companies raise again, and because not following on can leave you diluted. It is not an obligation. A new round is a new decision at a new price, and the first cheque is not an argument for the second.

Budget your time too. Each serious look takes hours, and each holding will ask for attention afterwards. The time you have limits how many holdings you can assess properly. In a syndicate, ask what the lead actually verified, how the lead gets paid and what you will legally hold.

Are you allowed in?

The rules depend on where you are and on the deal, but it pays to know the tests before someone asks.

In the UK, unapproved promotions can reach people who have signed a statement under the Financial Promotion Order. A high net worth statement requires income of £100,000 or more, or net assets of £250,000 or more excluding your home and pension. A sophisticated investor statement needs one of four things within its terms: two or more unlisted investments in the last two years; current membership of an angel network or syndicate, for more than six months; a professional role in private equity or small business finance in the last two years; or a directorship in that period of a company turning over at least £1 million.

Read the wording before you sign. You are stating that you can receive promotions which may not comply with FCA rules, that you can expect no protection from the FCA, the Financial Ombudsman Service or the Financial Services Compensation Scheme, and that you could lose everything.

In the US, the accredited investor tests for individuals include net worth above $1 million excluding your main home, or income above $200,000 ($300,000 jointly) in each of the last two years with the same expected this year.

UK tax relief changes the sum, not the risk

For UK taxpayers, EIS and SEIS can reduce the amount at stake. Income tax relief is 30% under EIS, on up to £1 million a year (£2 million if at least £1 million goes into knowledge-intensive companies), and 50% under SEIS on up to £200,000. On a £4,000 cheque that is £1,200 or £2,000 off your tax bill, if you have the tax to set against it.

The conditions matter. Shares must be newly issued and paid for in cash, and you must hold them for at least three years. You need the certificate from the company (EIS3 or SEIS3) before you can claim, so chase it. A company's eligibility also does not settle your own, and relief cannot turn a failing company into a good investment.

Read one document of each kind

You will meet two common forms. In a priced round you get shares at a stated price, so your percentage is set on the day. With a SAFE, you get a right to shares later. Under Y Combinator's post-money form, your starting percentage at the cap is the investment divided by the cap: $25,000 on a $5 million post-money cap is 0.5%. Later priced rounds still dilute it. The form was written for US companies, and other jurisdictions need their own versions. A UK deal may use something else, so read what is put in front of you.

For either form, find the share class, the liquidation preference and any rights to invest again. If you cannot explain what you would receive from a sale at half the current valuation, you do not yet understand the deal.

Keep the paper

From the first investment, hold one folder per company: signed agreement, proof of payment, evidence of your holding, tax certificate, and each update. Exits can take a decade. You will need these documents long after you have forgotten why you said yes, so add a short note of your reasons while you still remember them.
