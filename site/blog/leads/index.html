Leads and GPs

Running a syndicate or a first fund.

Articles

  • Raising a fund or staying deal by deal: the sums for a lead

    Fee income, carry and the long tail of vehicles work very differently in the two models. A lead should run the numbers before picking one.

  • Running a one-person investment firm: what to settle before the first close

    “Solo GP” describes who makes the decisions, not who does the work, and a one-person firm stands or falls on how the rest gets done.

  • Budget a first fund at its first close, not its target

    A first-time fund runs on the money that has actually closed, and a smaller figure changes the budget, the portfolio and the strategy.

  • How big must a winner be? Sizing a venture portfolio

    A few companies produce most venture returns, so a portfolio has to be built to catch one and to own enough of it to matter.

  • Follow-on reserves: what the next cheque has to earn

    A follow-on should be judged at today's price, on what the new money will return, and not on how well the first cheque has done.

  • Write an investment thesis you could lose an argument with

    A thesis earns its place when it rejects companies you would otherwise have liked, and when it can later be shown to be wrong.

  • Where your deals really come from: a sourcing log for syndicate leads

    A short log tells a syndicate lead which relationships produce investable deals, and where the rest fall away.

  • Reading a venture scout agreement before your first introduction

    “Scout” has no fixed legal meaning, so the agreement decides what you earn, what you may say and what you may do when the firm says no.

  • Assessing founders with evidence instead of charm

    Investors say the team matters most, but the checks that actually reveal a team are records, predictions and specific references.

  • How to test a network-effects claim in a pitch deck

    If half the users vanished tomorrow, would the rest be worse off? That question separates a network effect from ordinary growth.

  • Investing alongside an accelerator: what the badge changes

    An accelerator's stake may be bigger than the headline, its effect on the company is hard to measure, and its rights may reach your allocation.

  • Backing a venture studio spinout: what to check that a normal startup doesn't need

    When a company is built by a studio, the questions are who owns what, who pays whom and who is really running the business.

  • Checking a science claim you cannot judge yourself

    A syndicate lead does not need to out-think the founder on the science. The job is to find the one claim the price rests on and pay someone to try to break it.

  • When a portfolio company asks the SPV for more money

    A bridge request is a new investment decision with its own arithmetic. An SPV that has already spent its money also has a practical problem to solve before it can say yes.

  • How to test a fund administrator or system before you sign

    Sales demos show the deal that goes right, so bring the one that went wrong, then ask what would happen if you had to leave.
