UK nominee or Delaware partnership: start with the investor list

For a lead deciding where to set up a vehicle: the names on your investor list, more than the location of the company, usually narrow the choice.

Updated 7 November 2026

Bulma is raising for Senzu Labs. Goku, a UK taxpayer, wants tax relief. Piccolo files US tax returns. A handful of others sit in other countries. Before she compares providers or prices, she should go down that list and ask what each person needs from the vehicle, because the two common forms give them quite different things.

What each form gives an investor

Under a UK nominee or bare trust arrangement, the nominee is the legal holder and the investor owns the beneficial interest in the shares. For UK capital gains tax, the property is treated as the investor's own.

Under a Delaware limited partnership, the partnership holds the shares and the investor holds an interest in the partnership. A series structure is a further variant in which one partnership agreement establishes separate series with their own property, rights and obligations.

UK tax relief can settle it

If UK investors want EIS or SEIS relief, the structure may decide the matter. The EIS rules look through a nominee to the individual behind it. HMRC says that this look-through does not extend to investing in a partnership, since a partner owns a share of all the partnership's assets and not particular shares. A nominee does not guarantee relief, because the company, the share issue and the investor must each qualify. But a partnership closes off that route for Goku regardless.

US partnership reporting

For Piccolo, the question is paperwork as much as tax. A US partnership does not itself pay income tax. It passes profits and losses through to its partners, and it must give each of them a Schedule K-1. Investors file from those figures, so a late K-1 can hold up their own return, and the timing is worth asking about.

For a US investor in a UK nominee, the absence of a K-1 proves nothing. Ask how that investor's own adviser will classify and report the holding, and do not promise a simpler tax year than the facts support.

The rest of the list may need its own check. Tax transparency is a local verdict, so another country may tax the same vehicle as an entity, and income paid across borders can attract withholding. Ask early, using the actual countries on the list.

US offering and investor-count rules

If the vehicle is offering to US investors, three sets of rules matter, and they are separate.

The first concerns how the offer is made. Rule 506(b) bars general solicitation and allows up to 35 non-accredited but financially sophisticated investors in any 90-day period, with disclosure requirements attached. Rule 506(c) allows public marketing, but every purchaser must be accredited and the issuer must take reasonable steps to verify that.

The second concerns whether the vehicle is an investment company. Section 3(c)(1) generally excludes a vehicle beneficially owned by no more than 100 persons that makes no public offering. A qualifying venture capital fund may have 250, provided it meets the venture capital fund definition and holds no more than $12m in contributions and uncalled commitments. So a $9m venture vehicle with 120 investors might fit, while the same vehicle at $15m would not. Section 3(c)(7) takes a different approach, requiring all holders to be qualified purchasers. Counting rules can look through entities that invest, so an investing vehicle on the list may count as more than one.

The third concerns the sponsor. Who is advising the vehicle, and on what registration or exemption? Clearing one of these three hurdles says nothing about the other two.

Where the sponsor sits

Forming a vehicle in Delaware does not move the UK's rules out of the picture for a sponsor working from London. The UK ban on unapproved financial promotions applies to communications from outside the UK if they are capable of having an effect here. And managing assets with discretion is a specified activity that generally needs FCA permission when done by way of business in the UK, wherever the vehicle is formed. The analysis follows what the sponsor does and where, not the name on the certificate of formation.

Who decides at exit

Do not read decision rights off the jurisdiction. A UK nominee does not automatically need a majority vote; any threshold comes from its documents. A Delaware partnership does not automatically leave the sponsor free to act alone. The statute lets a partnership agreement remove the votes of a protected series' limited partners or grant them, on a per capita, financial-interest or other basis. Either structure can be drafted to give a sponsor more power or less, within the legal limits that apply to it. Check the actual wording, and check how it interacts with any drag-along in the company's shareholders' agreement.

The same care applies to the liability wall between series. Delaware protects a series from another's debts only if conditions are met: separate records for its assets, a provision in the partnership agreement, and the required notice in the certificate. Ask to see that the records are in fact kept separately.

When the list is mixed

Bulma's list contains both a relief-seeking UK taxpayer and a US filer. Her options are to pick one form and accept that someone's need goes unmet, or to run parallel vehicles side by side. Parallel vehicles cost more, and they call for a rule on keeping the two in step at the exit. A simple tally helps: how much of the raise comes from each group, and what would each lose under each form? When 90% of the money wants EIS relief, the answer usually becomes clear. When the money is split evenly, it may be worth the expense of doing both.
