Goten

The money work on an SPV, ready for a person to check

Much of the money work on an SPV still happens by hand. What Goten prepares at each step, from bank statement to exit, and what a person still decides.

Updated 7 November 2026

The same work on every deal

Much of the money work on an SPV or syndicate still happens by hand, in spreadsheets and email. Someone reads the bank statement line by line and matches each payment to an investor. They chase whoever is short. At the close they work out each investor’s investment, fees and refund, and the amount to wire to the company. At the exit they work out who gets what.

None of that is hard. It comes back on every deal, and the reconciliation grows with the number of investors.

Where it goes wrong

A payment arrives with its reference missing or mistyped, from a company account, short, or twice. An investor withdraws before the close. A fee is charged on the wrong base or to the wrong payer. Scale-back amounts do not add up to the round cap. At the exit, carry is taken before capital comes back, or a tier rate is applied to all the profit instead of its band.

Each step feeds the next. Book a payment to the wrong investor and two balances are wrong, and the error carries through the close to the exit. Once money has moved, a mistake is hard to undo.

What Goten prepares

You import the investor list and the bank statement, or type them in. Every result is worked out in exact whole pence and checks itself on the screen: for every investor, cash received must equal the amount invested plus fees plus refunds. If a blocking check fails, copying, exporting and the hand-off to the next step all stop.

One deal, worked through

Bulma is leading an SPV into Senzu Labs. Six investors owe £25,000 between them, and the bank statement is in. Goten has no connection to your bank: it works on the lines you give it.

Five payments match themselves

The statement says S GOKU. The commitment says Son Goku. The name does not decide anything here: the line carries SEN-001, the payment reference of exactly one investor, so Goten books it to that commitment. Four more lines do the same. A suspected duplicate would still wait for a person.

One does not

The remaining line says PICCOLO, £2,000, with no reference. Goten suggests Piccolo with a score of 0.55 and gives its reasons: the payer name matches and the amount matches.

A suggestion changes nothing. £23,000 is booked, £2,000 is unbooked, and the reconciliation cannot be carried to the close until someone decides. A line with no plausible candidate gets no suggestion: it stays unmatched and does not hold up the close, so someone has to look at it.

A person confirms it. Now £25,000 is booked and nothing is left over. That settles which commitment the money belongs to. Whether a payment from that account is acceptable is a separate check for your KYC and AML process, which Goten does not run.

Goten also writes the texts for this stage: a query asking a likely sender whether an unmatched payment is theirs, and a chase for an investor who is short. You copy them and send them yourself.

The close

A person carries the result to the closing allocation. With a 3% investor fee deducted from each payment, Goku’s £10,000 becomes £300 in fees and £9,700 invested. Piccolo’s confirmed £2,000 becomes £60 and £1,940. Across the deal, that is £750 in fees and £24,250 invested, and £24,250 is the amount to wire to the company. A person makes that wire: Goten moves no money.

The exit

A person carries the invested amounts to the exit. Say Senzu Labs is sold and this holding brings in £90,000, five years on, with £1,500 of costs, 20% carry, an 8% a year compounding hurdle and full catch-up.

Goten shows where each pound goes. Costs of £1,500.00 are paid first:

Step To investors To the lead
Return of capital £24,250.00
Preferred return (hurdle) £11,381.21
Lead catch-up £2,845.30
Carried interest split, 80 / 20 £40,018.79 £10,004.70
Total £75,650.00 £12,850.00

Piccolo’s £1,940 comes back as £6,052, carried from the payment a person confirmed at the start.

What your team gets

  • A ledger from the bank statement: every payment booked, suggested or unmatched, and every investor’s status.
  • Chase and query emails already written with the right amount, ready to copy.
  • Closing numbers that add up, the amount to wire and a confirmation for the lead.
  • An exit split that shows each step and its reason, down to each investor.
  • A record of the decisions behind those numbers, and files to export at each step.

What a person still does

Someone imports the investor list and the bank statement. Someone decides every uncertain payment, sends every message, gets every consent and makes every wire and refund. Goten calculates and explains. People act.

It has no connection to your bank and sends no email. It does not run KYC or AML, draft legal documents or make tax filings. It handles a nominee SPV with one currency, one investment and one exit, with the same terms for every investor. Staged contributions, earlier distributions, investor-specific carry, management fees and VAT are not covered. Withdrawals and fee payer changes work only before the close, and a fee payer change only for percentage fees. The activity log is a working record in this browser, not a regulatory ledger, and it is not tamper-proof.

Where the data is

Your deal data is processed in your browser and never sent by this app. There is no sign-up and no shared workspace: the deal stays in the browser where you entered it, so export what you need.

How it is checked

A separate implementation, written only from the rules, agrees with the app on hand-calculated cases and generated ones. The code and the release notes are public.
