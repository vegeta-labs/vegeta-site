Bulma

Start a deal and bring your investors in

Set up and submit your deal for review. Once approved, go live, invite investors and follow their progress from commitment to payment instructions.

Updated 23 November 2026

Before the money

Bulma is a syndicate lead setting up a deal for a company raising money, with investors she wants to invite. Before an investor can see payment instructions, they must complete the eligibility steps, choose an amount, pass an identity check and sign.

A costly mistake can come at the payment stage: an investor sending money to an account from an email they should not have trusted.

What Bulma — SPV Workspace does

Bulma takes a deal from set-up to each investor’s payment instructions. Bulma runs at an operator, whoever runs the SPVs: the operator sets the rules, reviews each deal and each identity check, and names the account investors pay into.

Bulma sets up the deal. A reviewer at the operator approves it before any investor can see it. Later changes go to a reviewer. Changes to what investors get, such as the price, carry or fees, always need committed investors to reconfirm; for other changes the reviewer decides whether they must reconfirm or are only told. Where the operator asks for content approval, an approver approves the change too. She then invites investors by link or by email, and investors who choose to proceed go through the required stages; the detailed eligibility steps depend on the operator’s rules:

Step What Bulma does What a person still does
Eligibility Asks the questions in the operator’s rules, and records each answer and statement The operator writes the rules and the wording
See the deal Shows the approved deal, only after the eligibility step Bulma writes it and a reviewer approves it
Commitment Shows the minimum, the fees and the exact amount to pay before the investor commits The investor chooses the amount
Identity check Sends the investor to the operator’s identity provider, or takes documents for a manual check A reviewer at the operator decides
Signing Prepares the investor’s own deal sheet and records what was signed, when and how The operator and its lawyers write the documents
Payment instructions Shows the amount still to pay, a payment reference and the account the operator names The operator checks its bank and records what arrived

An investor cannot sign until the identity check passes, and Bulma sees where each investor is.

Setting up the deal

In this example, she is taking an SPV into Senzu Labs. She fills in the deal in six parts: the basics, the company, the terms, the files, the economics and a review. She can move between them in any order; the review lists what is still missing.

She submits it. A reviewer at the operator approves it, sends it back with a reason, or rejects it. A reviewer cannot review a deal they lead, help run or earn carry on, or one from their own syndicate. If she changes anything while it waits, it goes back to the start of the review. Once the deal and its content are approved, she confirms it should go live.

Inviting investors

Where the operator allows links, each link carries its own terms: carry, and an optional fee. A link’s terms cannot be edited after it is created; to change them, she makes a new link. Terms that differ from the default need a reason, and where the operator’s rules require it, a reviewer approves them before the link can be used.

She can also invite by email. An email invitation works only for the address it was sent to, so a forwarded invitation does not let anyone else in. Neither the link nor the email shows the company, the amounts or the terms. An investor sees those only after passing the eligibility step.

What Goku sees before he pays

Goku is one of the investors Bulma invites. He opens his invitation, confirms he is eligible and reads the deal. He commits £10,000; in this example Bulma’s fee as lead is added on top. Before he confirms, Bulma shows him how the amount is made up: the commitment, her fee on his link, and the amount to pay in total. The amount to pay is exact; the expected investment is marked as an estimate until the deal closes.

Then he completes his identity check, and a reviewer at the operator decides it.

He signs his deal sheet. Then he sees his payment instructions: the amount still to pay, his own payment reference, the deadline and the account the operator names, with who holds it. He can download them as a PDF.

If his amount, his signed terms or the receiving account changes later, those instructions stop being valid at once. The PDF he downloaded cannot change, so Bulma emails him not to pay on the old instructions, and the check link in the PDF shows they are no longer valid. Bulma’s own emails never include bank details: those are shown only after he signs in.

Goku can report that he has sent his payment. Bulma is notified of his report; it does not mark the money as received, and only the operator records money received.

Following every investor

For investors who have accepted an invitation, Bulma’s investor list shows their status, who they are waiting on and how many days they have been at that step: To sign, waiting on the investor, four days. Verifying, waiting on a reviewer. To pay, waiting on the investor.

She sees the totals for the deal: committed, received, refunds due, refunded, with the operator, lead fees and what is usable for the deal. She can write to one investor, or, where the operator allows it, to a group. In the real mode someone at the operator approves the text before it goes, and messages sent through Bulma are kept on the site for the investor to read.

Only the operator records what has arrived: by hand, or by importing its bank lines. A lead cannot mark a payment as received.

What a person still does

The money goes to an account the operator names, and the operator checks what arrives. A reviewer decides every identity check; a result from the identity provider never decides anything by itself. The operator and its lawyers write the eligibility questions, the deal sheet and the text investors read about their money.

Bulma does not move money, hold shares or run a wallet. It does not decide who is eligible beyond the operator’s rules, does not give advice, and has no public list of deals: investors reach one only through an invitation. It does not take on the operator’s regulatory duties, and switching to the real mode needs the operator’s own regulatory arrangements and legal advice.

This first version does not handle the closing itself: she can ask to close, but nothing takes the deal on to completion. It is built for one operator running primary deals through a UK bare trust, and cannot be used by appointed representatives.

Where it runs

Bulma runs on the operator’s own server, with Docker. It starts as a sandbox with fictional data, and no email leaves it. The real mode needs the operator’s own email server settings, its legal wording, payment instructions from a file, and at least three different people: two admins and a reviewer, with someone named to approve promotions. Identity checks use the operator’s own Sumsub contract or manual checks. A second admin approves the switch, and there is no way back.

How it was tested

Checked on the release branch, run locally: 1,130 of 1,130 unit and real-database tests and 40 of 40 browser tests passed, and all 185 test-plan cases due in this release have a passing test. A full mutation run broke the code on purpose 1,477 times, and the tests caught every one. A Python oracle, written separately from the code, matched on 35,084 of 35,084 random and boundary cases. The release notes have the details.
