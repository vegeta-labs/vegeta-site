After the exit: paying out and closing a UK SPV

Paying the last pound and ending the vehicle can take a year longer than the sale, and the steps depend on whether there is a company to close.

Updated 27 November 2026

Explore

When Senzu Labs is sold, Bulma's investors expect one payment. Her deal produces two, a year apart, and then a closing task that differs between the two common UK shapes: a nominee holding shares on bare trust, and an SPV that is itself a limited company.

The last distribution is rarely the first one

Goku, Piccolo, Krillin and Tien paid in £120,000, £90,000, £60,000 and £30,000: £300,000 in all. Bulma's carry is 20% of profit.

The buyer pays £1,000,000 for the syndicate's shares, but £100,000 of it sits in escrow for twelve months in case the buyer makes a claim under the sale agreement. So £900,000 arrives. Bulma pays £10,000 of exit costs and holds back £6,000 for closing costs.

First payment Second payment Total
Received £900,000 £80,000 £980,000
Exit costs (£10,000) (£10,000)
Closing reserve held or released (£6,000) £1,500 (£4,500)
Distributed £884,000 £81,500 £965,500
Carry to Bulma £116,800 £16,300 £133,100
To investors £767,200 £65,200 £832,400

The table leaves out any tax the vehicle itself owes. A year later a £20,000 claim is settled out of escrow and £80,000 is released. Closing cost £4,500, so £1,500 of the reserve is paid out.

The carry calculation should be run on the running total, not on each payment alone. Capital went back in the first payment, so all £81,500 of the second is profit. Goku's 40% share is £306,880 and then £26,080, or £332,960 in total. Tell investors at the first payment how much is held back, why, and when it is due. The walk from sale price down to the SPV's receipt is in Following one cheque.

Shape one: a nominee and a bare trust

Here there is no deal-specific company. A nominee holds the shares, and the investors own the value behind it. Nothing has to be dissolved. The trust has done its work when the nominee holds nothing more for the investors.

That is not the day of the first payment. Until the escrow is released, the nominee still holds a right to money for them. Take the end date from the documents.

If the trust was registered with HMRC, the trustee closes it through the online service, giving the date the trust ended. That is a must where the trust was registered under the money laundering rules. The same page says changes to a trust that is not liable to tax must be reported within 90 days.

For capital gains tax, what passes between a nominee and the person it holds for is disregarded, so the disposal that counts is the sale itself. How escrowed money is taxed, and in which year, is a question for each investor's adviser.

Shape two: the SPV is a company

Now there is a legal person to end, and there are two routes.

Voluntary strike-off. The directors, or a majority of them, apply to the registrar on form DS01. The application cannot be made if in the previous three months the company changed its name, traded, sold property it held for resale, or did anything else beyond what is needed to conclude its affairs, make the application or meet a legal requirement. Nor, GOV.UK adds, if it is threatened with liquidation or has an agreement with creditors. A copy of the application goes to every member, creditor, employee and non-signing director within seven days. The registrar publishes a notice in the Gazette, and after two months may strike the company off; it is dissolved when the second notice appears.

Members' voluntary liquidation. A majority of directors make a declaration of solvency: after full inquiry, they believe the company can pay its debts in full, with interest at the official rate, within a stated period of no more than 12 months from the start of the winding up. Shareholders then pass the winding-up resolution within five weeks and appoint an authorised insolvency practitioner as liquidator. The company is dissolved three months after the liquidator's final account is registered. A director who makes the declaration without reasonable grounds commits an offence.

Empty the company first

Whatever a company still owns at dissolution belongs to the Crown as bona vacantia, or in some areas to the Duchy of Lancaster or the Duke of Cornwall. GOV.UK says this includes bank balances and future payments such as tax refunds, and the government's guidance lists the benefit of contracts too.

If Bulma's SPV were a company struck off before the escrow ended, the right to the £80,000 would be the Crown's, and getting it back would start with restoring the company. An application to court for restoration generally has to be made within six years of dissolution. So the order is: escrow settled, tax paid, last distribution made, bank account at nil, then the application.

One exception matters to nominees. Property a company holds on trust for another person does not pass to the Crown. That still leaves investors' assets in the name of a company that no longer exists, so a nominee company should not be dissolved while it holds anything.

Tax depends on the route

The company must send final accounts and a Company Tax Return to HMRC and pay what it owes before strike-off.

For shareholders, the route changes the character of the money. A distribution made in a winding up is generally not treated as an income distribution, and a capital distribution is treated as a disposal of an interest in the shares. An anti-avoidance rule can treat it as income where all of its conditions are met. In outline: the individual has at least 5% of the ordinary shares and the votes that go with them; the company is a close company or was one in the previous two years; within two years the same or a similar activity is carried on by the individual, or through a partnership, company or connected person the section names; and avoiding income tax is a main purpose. The section has further detail and exclusions. Going from one SPV to the next does not meet that test on its own, but it is the pattern to raise with an adviser. Before a strike-off, the same treatment is available only where the company's debts are dealt with and the total distributed is no more than £25,000. Above that, GOV.UK says the amount will be treated as income. HMRC's manual adds that the company must actually be dissolved within two years of the distribution.

Whatever an SPV company has left after its own tax, a payout of this size is far above that limit. Settle the route, and how the first payment is made, with a tax adviser before any money leaves the company.

What to keep, and for how long

  • Company accounting records: company law has a private company preserve them for three years from the date they are made, and tax law requires six years, which GOV.UK counts from the end of the financial year they relate to.
  • After strike-off: GOV.UK says to keep business documents for seven years.
  • A paid trustee that is itself subject to the money laundering rules must keep its beneficial ownership records for five years after the final distribution.

The closing folder should hold the completion statement, the escrow release, both distribution schedules tied to the bank statements, and the filing that ended the vehicle. Goten, Vegeta's deal calculations module, exports the distribution as a CSV with one row per investor, and because a deal is stored only in the browser, that file is the copy to keep.
