Investor onboarding records: what to keep, who keeps it, and when it goes

Onboarding a syndicate investor leaves five kinds of record behind, and each has its own owner, its own purpose and its own end date.

Updated 27 November 2026

Explore

Bulma is raising £100,000 for Senzu Labs from four investors: Goku £40,000, Piccolo £25,000, Krillin £20,000 and Tien £15,000. A regulated firm acts as nominee and runs the checks. By the time money moves, passport scans, signed statements, subscription documents, bank details and emails sit across Bulma's laptop, the firm's systems and an identity provider's dashboard. Nobody has written down which copies must exist and which should not.

Five records, five purposes

Record Why it exists Who needs it
Identity and verification evidence Customer due diligence The firm with the legal duty
Investor statement The promotion exemption Whoever made the communication
Subscription documents The contract and the holding The parties, and the nominee
Bank details Refunds and distributions Whoever sends payments
Correspondence What was said and agreed Each side, as far as needed

Personal data must be adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary for its purpose. If you cannot name the purpose, you should not be holding the record.

Who the Money Laundering Regulations apply to

The Regulations apply to "relevant persons" acting in the course of business in the UK. The list is closed: it includes credit and financial institutions, accountants, independent legal professionals, and trust or company service providers. "Syndicate lead" is not on it.

That does not put every lead outside. A trust or company service provider includes a firm or sole practitioner who, by way of business, forms companies, or acts, or arranges for another person to act, as a nominee shareholder. A lead who sets up vehicles and arranges nominees for a fee should ask a lawyer which side of that line they are on, and file the answer.

In the example, assume the nominee firm is the relevant person and Bulma only introduces investors. Whether a given lead is a relevant person turns on the definitions in regulations 10 and 12 and is a question for a lawyer. It must identify each investor and verify their identity when it establishes a business relationship. On that assumption Bulma has no due diligence duty of her own, and so no purpose under that heading for holding four passport scans. Data she has no purpose for is data the minimisation rule says she should not hold.

Software is not on the list either. Duties fall on persons in business and, in data protection law, on the controller, who decides the purposes and means of the processing. Whoever runs a program answers for what is in it.

How long the firm keeps due diligence records

Regulation 40 requires a relevant person to keep a copy of the documents and information obtained for customer due diligence, with enough supporting records to reconstruct a transaction. The period is five years, beginning when the firm knows or has reasonable grounds to believe the business relationship has ended. For records of individual transactions inside a continuing relationship, the regulation says they need not be kept for more than ten years.

The clock starts at the end, not at onboarding. Suppose a final distribution on 30 June 2031 ends the firm's relationship with Goku, Piccolo and Krillin. Their records must be kept at least until the end of June 2036, nearly ten years after the passports were uploaded.

Once the period expires, the firm must delete personal data obtained for the purposes of the Regulations, unless another enactment or court proceedings require it, the person has consented, or the firm reasonably believes it is needed for legal proceedings.

Now take Tien. He passes the checks, then withdraws on 10 December 2026 and never pays. If the firm treats the relationship as ended that day, his five years run out in December 2031, more than four years before the others. One deal has two deletion dates, so keep the schedule per investor, not per deal.

The records with no fixed period

For the rest, the UK GDPR sets no specific time limits. The ICO says you must be able to justify how long you keep data, review it periodically, and erase or anonymise what you no longer need. That guidance is marked as under review after the Data (Use and Access) Act, so read it again before relying on it.

Statements. The high net worth exemption depends on a statement signed within the twelve months ending with the day of the communication, and the sophisticated investor article uses the same words. Goku signed on 2 March 2026. A memo sent to him on 1 March 2027 is inside the twelve months; one sent on 2 March 2027 is not. The person who made the communication needs the signed statement, its date, and the date of each communication it supported.

Subscription documents. They prove what each investor agreed and holds, so they are needed while the holding lasts. After that, one reference point is the six years for bringing an action on a simple contract in England and Wales. Deeds, tax records and company records have their own rules.

Bank details. They exist to pay an account that has been checked with its owner. Hold one current, checked set per investor as the live payment instruction. Superseded details leave the live record, but the evidence of past payments and of each change request stays with the transaction records for their own period.

Correspondence. Keep messages that record a decision, an instruction or a disclosure. An investor can ask for erasure of data that is no longer necessary, but not of data needed to comply with a legal obligation or defend legal claims.

What a "pass" replaces, and what it does not

Electronic checks are recognised. Information may count as coming from a reliable, independent source where it is obtained through an electronic identification process that is secure from fraud and gives enough assurance that the person is who they claim to be.

Three things stay with the firm.

  • Liability. It may apply its checks through an agent or outsourcing provider, but only on terms under which it remains liable for any failure.
  • The rest of due diligence. Besides verifying identity, the firm must assess the purpose and intended nature of the relationship, and be able to show its supervisor that its measures fit the risk.
  • The records. Regulation 40 speaks of a copy of the documents and information obtained. A green tick is a result. Ask whether the evidence behind it can be exported, and what happens to it if the provider contract ends in 2029 while the retention period runs to 2036.

A one-page schedule

For each investor, list the five records, where each is stored, who is responsible, the event that starts its clock, and the deletion date once that event has happened. Then look for stray copies in inboxes and shared drives. Bulma's schedule has four investors, five records each, and no passport scans of her own.

Bulma, Vegeta's SPV workspace, has a setting for how long records are kept and removes personal data when that period ends.
