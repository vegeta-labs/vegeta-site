SEIS and EIS through a syndicate: what the relief is worth and when it arrives

UK tax relief can change the worst case on an early-stage investment, but it comes off a tax bill, it depends on paperwork the investor does not control, and it can be withdrawn for three years.

Updated 7 November 2026

Bulma is leading an SPV into Senzu Labs, and Goku is putting in £20,000. He has two questions: how much of that comes back, and when? The answers depend on which UK scheme the share issue falls under, on Goku's own tax position, and on a company filing neither of them submits.

The relief reduces tax you already owe

The Seed Enterprise Investment Scheme gives 50% Income Tax relief on up to £200,000 a tax year; the Enterprise Investment Scheme gives 30% on up to £1 million, or £2 million if at least £1 million of that goes into knowledge-intensive companies. So Goku's £20,000 is worth £10,000 of relief under SEIS or £6,000 under EIS.

That is a ceiling, not a payment. Relief is set against UK Income Tax he owes, and anything unused cannot be carried forward. If his bill for the year is £7,000, he can use £7,000 of the £10,000 in that year. He can instead elect to treat some or all of the investment as made in the previous tax year, which helps if that year's bill was larger. Relief is worked out on the amount subscribed for shares, so a fee paid to run the vehicle would not normally be part of the figure.

The company's situation decides the scheme

Investors do not choose. The share issue either fits a scheme or it does not.

Test SEIS EIS (most companies, from 6 April 2026)
Money raised £250,000 maximum, counting other de minimis state aid from the previous 3 years £10 million in 12 months, £24 million lifetime
Gross assets £350,000 at issue £30 million before, £35 million after
Employees Fewer than 25 Fewer than 250
Age Qualifying trade no more than 3 years old Normally within 7 years of first commercial sale

Knowledge-intensive companies have higher limits, and Northern Ireland companies trading in goods or wholesale electricity stay on lower ones.

Order matters when a round uses both. A company must not have received EIS or VCT investment up to and including the day SEIS shares are issued, so today the SEIS shares go in at least a day earlier. HMRC published a proposal in October 2026 to allow same-day issues from 6 April 2027, to be legislated in Finance Bill 2026-27.

What the worst case looks like

If Senzu Labs fails, the loss is not the full £20,000. A loss on these shares can be set against income for the year of the loss or the year before, less the Income Tax relief already given. Assume Goku kept his relief, meets the share loss conditions and has enough income taxed at 40% to absorb the loss.

SEIS EIS
Subscription £20,000 £20,000
Income Tax relief £10,000 £6,000
Loss left to claim £10,000 £14,000
Tax saved on that loss at 40% £4,000 £5,600
Money actually lost £6,000 £8,400

Two details catch people out. The usual cap on Income Tax reliefs does not apply to share loss relief on EIS or SEIS shares. And a low valuation is not a claim: there has to be a disposal, or a negligible value claim made while he still owns shares that have become worthless, with the loss claim made within a year of the 31 January after the tax year of the loss.

The upside has its own rules. Shares held for the minimum period are exempt from Capital Gains Tax on sale if Income Tax relief was received and not withdrawn. EIS can also defer a gain from another asset, where the investment falls between one year before and three years after that sale. SEIS can exempt half of a reinvested gain, up to £100,000 of relief on £200,000 invested. Deferral only postpones the gain; reinvestment relief removes part of it.

Why the certificate takes months

Goku cannot claim until he holds a compliance certificate, SEIS3 or EIS3, and that depends on the company.

For SEIS, the company can only file its compliance statement once it has traded for at least four months or spent at least 70% of the money raised. For EIS, it has to wait until it has carried on the qualifying activity for four months, and then has up to two years from that point, or from the end of the tax year of issue if later. HMRC then authorises the statement and issues a unique investment reference and the certificate for investors.

So the useful answer to "where is my certificate?" is a date: when the company expects to hit the four-month or 70% mark, and whether the statement has gone in. A company with advance assurance has HMRC's view that the proposed investment is likely to qualify, based on what it told HMRC. That is not a certificate, it does not assess any individual investor, and it stops applying if changes are not disclosed when the statement is filed.

Once the certificate arrives there is no rush. Goku has up to five years after the 31 January following the tax year of investment. For the current year he can ask for a tax code or payment-on-account change; for an earlier year he claims through Self Assessment.

What the SPV changes

A nominee does not break the chain. Shares subscribed for and held through a nominee are treated as the individual's own, and the SEIS3 should show the actual subscriber. The EIS rule works the same way.

One structure needs care. If shares are issued to a single bare trust for two or more beneficiaries, each beneficiary is treated as having subscribed an equal share of the total, whatever they actually paid (SEIS, EIS). Syndicate stakes are rarely equal, so how each investor's shares are held has to be settled with an adviser before the shares are issued.

A partnership does break the chain: HMRC says a partner, including in a limited partnership or LLP, owns a proportion of all the partnership's assets rather than specific shares, so the nominee rule does not reach them.

For Bulma, this makes the investor list a tax document. HMRC's manual allows that a company may not know who stands behind a nominee, but each investor files their own claim and needs a certificate that supports it, so give the company each person's name and exact subscription. A name or amount that is wrong in that list can end up wrong on the certificate too.

What can undo the relief

The conditions run for at least three years, and some of them turn on other people.

Relief can be lost if an investor sells, receives money or assets from the company, or becomes connected with it, or if the company stops qualifying. Connection covers being employed by the company, or holding more than 30% of its shares, votes or asset rights, and it counts associates such as a spouse, parents, children and business partners. Directors differ by scheme: SEIS allows them, while EIS bars paid directors unless the payment is a permitted one. Anyone caught by one of these events has 60 days to tell HMRC.

Most of these risks reach the investor through decisions taken elsewhere, such as a share reorganisation, a founder's relative joining the round or a buyback offer. A lead who sees such a proposal during the three years should ask how it affects relief before anyone signs.
