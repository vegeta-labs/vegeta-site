A second close: letting investors in after the shares have been issued

In a UK nominee SPV a later close is a new share issue with its own date, price and paperwork, and the work is in keeping the investor schedule equal to the company's register.

Updated 27 November 2026

Explore

Bulma's syndicate closed its investment in Senzu Labs in March. Goku put in £60,000 and Piccolo £40,000, at £2.00 a share. Six weeks later the company's round is still open, and Krillin and Tien want to come in with £30,000 and £20,000. The company is willing.

What a second close is here

In a deal-by-deal SPV held through a nominee, each investor owns particular shares, held for them on bare trust. A second close does not re-cut a pool. Senzu Labs issues more shares to the same nominee, and those new shares belong to Krillin and Tien.

A fund works differently. Its later investors usually buy into what has already happened, often paying interest so that everyone ends up level. A nominee SPV has nothing to level, because Goku's shares never become partly Krillin's.

The register and the schedule

Senzu Labs' register of members must state the shares held by each member, and for a company registered in England and Wales or Northern Ireland no notice of any trust may be entered on it. So the register still has one line for the nominee. Only the number on it changes.

Investor Paid for shares Shares Of the nominee's line before After
Goku £60,000 30,000 60.00% 40.00%
Piccolo £40,000 20,000 40.00% 26.67%
Krillin £30,000 15,000 20.00%
Tien £20,000 10,000 13.33%
Nominee's line £150,000 75,000 50,000 shares 75,000 shares

Goku still has 30,000 shares. His slice of the nominee's holding has fallen from 60% to 40% only because the holding grew. Record each investor as a share count with an issue date and a price paid. Treat the percentage as a figure worked out from those counts, and never store it as the entitlement itself.

The fall that does matter to Goku is at the company. If Senzu Labs had 1,000,000 shares in issue after the first close, his 30,000 were 3.00% of it. The 25,000 new shares take the total to 1,025,000 and his stake to 2.93%. That would have happened whoever bought the new shares.

Do late investors pay the same price?

The company and the terms of its round decide that. If the round is still open on its original terms, Krillin and Tien pay £2.00 like everyone else. If the company has moved the price to £2.50, Krillin's £30,000 buys 12,000 shares instead of 15,000. Nothing in the nominee structure prevents two prices, because the schedule holds a count and a price for each person. It does mean a sale at one price per share gives different gains to investors who paid different prices.

Costs are a separate question. Suppose the vehicle's fixed costs are £3,000, charged on top of each investment in proportion to it. Across the first close that was 3%: £1,800 from Goku and £1,200 from Piccolo. Across £150,000 it is 2%: £1,200, £800, £600 and £400. If the documents say costs are shared across everyone in the vehicle, Goku is owed £600 and Piccolo £400, paid for by what Krillin and Tien contribute. If they say each close bears its own costs, nobody is owed anything. Either rule can be fair. Write it down before the first close.

What must be settled before the shares are issued

The company's authority. Directors of a private company with only one class of shares may generally allot further shares of that class under the Act itself, unless its articles prohibit it. Otherwise directors may allot shares if they are authorised by the articles or by a resolution, and an authorisation under that section must state a maximum number of shares. Existing shareholders also have a statutory right to be offered new shares first, which a private company's articles may exclude. Ask the company's lawyers to confirm that the approvals given for the first close cover another 25,000 shares.

The money. Where the round is under EIS, the shares must be fully paid up at the time they are issued. HMRC's guidance is that the cash must be paid in full by the time the shares are issued. So Krillin's and Tien's payments are matched to the bank statement and sent to the company first.

How the shares are held. The rule that treats beneficiaries of a single bare trust as having subscribed equal slices, covered in the bare trust article, applies to the second issue just as it did to the first. Settle with the nominee and a tax adviser how Krillin's and Tien's shares are held before the company issues them.

The scheme. A second close is a second share issue. The company completes a new compliance statement for each share issue, and each issue has its own dates. Where the company was already carrying on its qualifying trade, the three-year period runs from each issue, so it ends later for Krillin's and Tien's shares than for Goku's; where the trade started after the shares were issued, it is counted from the start of the trade instead. One trap is specific to a later close. As the rules stand in November 2026, once a company has issued EIS shares it cannot issue shares under SEIS, so if the first close was EIS, a late investor cannot be offered SEIS shares. A proposed change to the ordering rule is covered in SEIS and EIS through a syndicate.

The dates to write down

Company law treats shares as allotted when a person acquires the unconditional right to be included in the register. HMRC's view is that shares are issued when the holder's title is complete, which normally means the entry in the register of members, and that a share certificate is only evidence. Those can be different days, and the EIS periods are set by reference to the issue, one of them starting before it. Record each issue date and the end date that follows from it.

After the allotment the company must register it as soon as practicable and within two months, deliver a return of allotment within one month on form SH01, and have the share certificate ready within two months unless the conditions of issue provide otherwise. These are the company's duties, but the lead should ask for the evidence. If the trust is on HMRC's register, new beneficiaries are a change to report, and for a non-taxable trust changes must be reported within 90 days.

What to tell the first investors

Tell them before the second close. Their share count and price have not changed. The nominee's holding has grown from 50,000 shares to 75,000, so their percentage of it is smaller. Their stake in Senzu Labs is smaller too, because the company issued more shares. If costs are being rebalanced, say what each person is owed and when it will be paid. If the trust terms count votes by holding, say that the arithmetic of any majority has changed as well.

Then send each of the four an updated statement, and check that the counts on those statements add up to 75,000, the number on Senzu Labs' register.
