Following one cheque from closing to exit

How a £12m purchase and a £22m sale turn into one investor's 1.79x, and which documents explain each step.

Updated 7 November 2026

When a company bought for £12m is sold for £22m, the press does the obvious sum and gets 1.83x. Goku, who put £375,000 into the deal through an SPV, gets 1.79x. Nobody has made a mistake. The two figures measure different things, and an investor who can walk from one to the other can read almost any deal pack.

Here is the deal. Senzu Labs Holdings is a new company set up to buy a contract manufacturer. Bulma leads an SPV that invests in the holding company alongside other investors. There is one class of ordinary shares, no later funding round, and the sums ignore tax and timing.

The price is one line of the budget

The purchase price is rarely the amount that has to be raised. A sources and uses table shows the whole bill on the left and who pays it on the right, and the two sides must match to the pound.

Uses £m Sources £m
Purchase price 12.0 Bank loan 5.0
Advisers and legal costs 0.6 Seller's shares (rollover) 1.0
Integration budget 0.9 New equity 8.0
Cash left in the business 0.5
Total 14.0 Total 14.0

Two lines deserve a second look.

The seller is taking £1m of the price in holding company shares. That lowers the cash paid out to £11m, but it brings no money in. Strip it from both sides and the table still balances: £13m of cash uses against £13m of cash from the bank and new investors. A plan that treats rollover as spare cash for integration is short by exactly that amount.

The equity cheque is £8m, not the £7m you get by subtracting the loan from the price. Costs, integration and the cash buffer account for the extra £1m of new money, even after the seller's rollover has reduced what must be found.

A percentage of a percentage

Holding company equity now totals £9m: £8m of new money plus the seller's £1m. Bulma's SPV contributes £3.6m of the new money, which buys 40%.

The SPV raises slightly more than it invests. It collects £3.75m from its investors and keeps £150,000 for its own set-up costs and running expenses. Goku's £375,000 is 10% of the SPV.

That gives him a look-through interest of 10% of 40%, or 4% of the holding company. It is a useful number, but he does not own those shares. The SPV owns them, the SPV's documents set out what he is entitled to, and any vote passes through whoever controls the SPV. A one-page diagram of the entities, with a percentage on every link, tells you which level any quoted ownership figure belongs to.

The cash he has at work in the company is also lower than the cheque he wrote: 10% of £3.6m is £360,000. The other £15,000 is his share of the SPV's costs.

From sale price to equity

Three years later the group is sold for an enterprise value of £22m. Enterprise value prices the business before its debt and cash are dealt with, so the bridge to shareholders runs like this:

£m
Enterprise value 22.0
Less bank debt outstanding (3.5)
Plus cash in the business 0.7
Less sale costs (0.4)
Equity proceeds 18.8

Set against the £9m of equity invested, that is about 2.09x. It is a better figure than the press's 1.83x, because debt financed part of the purchase and has been partly paid down. Leverage works in the other direction too: had the sale come in at £11m, equity would have received £7.8m and shown a loss, on a sale only £1m below the purchase price.

A real completion statement will add adjustments for working capital and may hold money back in escrow. Those change the amount and the date, not the shape of the walk. Any preference shares would also come out at this stage, ahead of the ordinary shares.

From equity to the investor

The SPV's 40% brings in £7.52m. After £50,000 of exit costs, it has £7.47m to distribute.

Now the SPV's own waterfall applies. In this deal, investors first get back the £3.75m they contributed, leaving £3.72m of profit. Bulma's carried interest is 20% of that profit, or £744,000. Investors share the remaining £6.726m, and Goku's 10% is £672,600.

On his £375,000, that is 1.79x.

Measure Calculation Multiple
Company price £22m ÷ £12m 1.83x
Holding company equity £18.8m ÷ £9m 2.09x
Goku's cheque £672,600 ÷ £375,000 1.79x

A deal pack that quotes the middle number without saying so overstates what this SPV investor received by about 0.3x. Waterfall terms vary, too: some carry sits above a hurdle, and some SPVs charge fees that come off before anything is shared.

What to ask for before closing

Test the deal model by changing the exit price. If it is built properly, every line down to a single investor's distribution moves with it. If the closing budget, the SPV's costs and the carry calculation live in three separate documents that have never been tied together, the first full reconciliation will happen at exit, when any disagreement is expensive.

After closing, keep the same chain current. Every debt repayment, new share issue or expense paid out of the SPV changes the answer for someone at the bottom of it.
