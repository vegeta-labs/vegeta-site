How it works

Step 1 · Set up

The lead sets up the deal. The operator reviews it.

Terms, memo and documents go to a reviewer. Nothing goes live until it is approved and the lead confirms.

Step 2 · Invite

Only invited investors see the deal.

By link or by email. Each investor confirms eligibility, reads the deal and commits, as themselves or through a company or trust. Then an identity check, which a reviewer decides.

Lead, investor, operator

For the lead

  • Set up a deal and send it for review.
  • Invite by link or by email; a link can carry its own terms where the operator allows it.
  • See each investor’s status, who it waits on and for how many days.
  • Email investors, propose a new amount or change terms.

For the investor

  • Confirm eligibility, then read the key terms, the memo and the documents.
  • Commit as yourself or through a company or trust, and pass an identity check.
  • Sign and get payment instructions.
  • Keep the signed deal sheet and any payment details still valid.

Run by the operator

  • Reviews every deal before investors see it, and most changes to a live deal.
  • Reviews identity checks; a provider’s result decides nothing by itself.
  • Sets eligibility steps and legal text in its own regime pack.
  • Records money received. Every change is in the audit log.

Limits

It never moves money.

It shows investors where to pay. The money goes to an account the operator names, and the operator records what arrives.

Money and shares
Bulma never moves money, holds shares or runs a wallet.
Advice
It gives no investment, tax or legal advice, and it does not judge S/EIS eligibility or whether the operator meets its legal duties.
Who sees a deal
You can see a deal only if you were invited, and only after a few checks.
The real mode
Switching to the real mode needs the operator’s own regulatory arrangements and legal advice. A second admin approves the switch, and there is no way back to the sandbox.
Appointed representatives
An appointed representative cannot use this version.
The operator’s duties
This software does not take on the operator’s regulatory duties: get your own legal advice first. Sanctions and PEP screening are outside this software.

FAQ

Is it for me?

Who runs it?

An operator: a firm that runs SPV deals and installs it on its own server. Leads and investors use it in the browser, with an account the operator’s instance holds.

Can a lead use it without an operator?

No. The operator reviews every deal before it goes live, and the real mode needs the operator’s own regulatory status.

Which deals does it support?

UK bare trust SPVs: equity, convertible loan notes, SAFEs or advance subscription agreements in a company’s round, or fund units.

For investors

What is an investor asked to do?

Create an account and complete the operator’s eligibility steps, such as a statement, a short test and a risk warning. Then read the deal and commit, pass an identity check, and sign. Payment instructions come after signing.

Can I invest through a company or trust?

Yes. You need proof of identity and address for whoever invests: you, or the company or trust. A company or trust also lists its people: directors, anyone with a share over 25% or in control, and whoever acts for it. Each beneficial owner confirms their details through their own link.

Who sees my documents?

The operator’s reviewers, on the operator’s own server. The lead sees how far the checks have got, not the documents. If the operator uses Sumsub, Sumsub holds what it collects under the operator’s contract.

Running it

How do I try it?

With Docker Compose on your own machine. It starts as a sandbox, and no email leaves it.

What does the real mode need?

A regulatory status, the name of the operator’s accountable person, your own regime pack with no placeholder text, two admins and a reviewer who are three different people, at least one promotion approver, and payment instructions from a file. A second admin approves the switch.

Can I use my own identity-check provider?

Sumsub is built in, under your own contract. The manual route works without any provider.

Risk and status

Does it move money?

No. It shows the payment instructions the operator set. The money goes to an account the operator names, and the operator records what arrives.

How do you know it works?

The money rules are compared with an independent Python implementation on random cases, and the main flows are tested in a real browser. The main figures are on this page; the release notes give every result.

What state is it in?

It is version 0.1.0: only what is built and tested is described here, and the release notes list its limits. It starts as a sandbox with fictional data, and no email leaves it; the real mode needs the operator’s own email server, legal wording and payment details. Read who does what before you run it for real.
