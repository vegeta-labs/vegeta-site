FAQ

About Vegeta

What is Vegeta?

Vegeta is open-source software for syndicate leads, the investors they invite and the teams that run SPVs. Each module handles a specific part of the work. Bulma — SPV Workspace takes invited investors from eligibility to payment instructions, on your own server. Goten — Deal Calculations reconciles receipts, works out the closing allocation and splits an exit, in your browser. Trunks — Operations Inbox prepares each email in your operations mailbox for a person to review, on your own server.

Is it free?

Yes. All three modules are open source under the Apache-2.0 licence. Goten needs no sign-up. For Bulma and Trunks you pay only for your own server and the providers you choose: email and identity checks for Bulma, a hosted model for Trunks if you use one.

Is it a regulated service?

No. Vegeta is open-source software, not a regulated service. It does not hold money or give advice. In Goten and Trunks a person checks every result and makes every decision. In Bulma, the rules the operator sets decide who can take each step, and a person at the operator decides each identity check.

Data and trust

Where does my data go?

Goten: Your deal data is processed in your browser and never sent by this app. The hosting provider still receives normal page requests. Trunks: it runs on your own server and sends only redacted text to the model provider you configure, or, with a local model, keeps the model traffic inside your network (what is sent to the model). Bulma: it runs on the operator’s own server, which keeps investors’ details and documents; if the operator uses Sumsub, Sumsub holds what it collects under the operator’s contract.

How do the modules fit together?

They are separate tools. Trunks can import your deals, the Goten commitments file, your bank statement and the reconciliation ledger exported from Goten as CSV files, read-only, and it suggests actions such as marking an investor withdrawn in Goten. A person makes the change.

How is it tested?

Each module page shows the figures from the release checks that measured them: Bulma, Goten and Trunks. All three compare their rules with an independent Python implementation and use mutation checks. Trunks is also measured on emails that development never read, with a real model, before each release that changes how emails are judged.
